2 Critical Fixes P0 5 items

Not features — defects. These sit in live code paths and should be closed before the system holds real employee data.

IDItemWhy it mattersEffort
P0.1Permanent attendance retentionDeleting an employee currently erases their entire attendance history permanently. Records are evidence in pay and labour disputes and must survive employee removal.0.5 d
P0.2Soft delete / archiveRemoving an employee, department or office destroys the record outright. Archiving supports rehire and historical reporting.1 d
P0.3Login brute-force protectionUnlimited password attempts are currently possible. Rate limiting and lockout is a baseline security control.0.5 d
P0.4Production configurationDebug mode exposes internal errors and database details to any visitor who triggers a failure.0.5 d
P0.5Branded error pagesUsers currently see raw framework screens on 403, 404 and 500 errors.0.5 d
P0.6Employee login creationThere is no way to give an employee a login from the dashboard — the record has no account or password field. Every employee added since setup is unable to sign in, which makes the self-service portal and the future mobile app unreachable for them. See A3.5.1 d
Total ≈ 4 days. The highest-value four days available anywhere in this catalogue.

One standing caveat. The full programme is roughly one developer-year on a codebase with no automated test coverage. The risk is not in any individual feature — it is in expanding an untested foundation. Item C1.11 should be funded before the expansion, not after it.

21 Annex — Required Accounts & Services

External accounts, subscriptions and assets the project depends on. Nothing here is written by the development team — each item must be registered, purchased or supplied by the organisation. Items are grouped by the stage at which they become blocking.

Register everything under a company identity, never a personal one. Store accounts, Firebase projects and hosting must be owned by a company email address controlled by the organisation. If a developer registers the Google Play or Apple account personally, transferring it later is slow, sometimes impossible, and the organisation cannot publish updates without that person. This is the single most common and most expensive mistake in mobile projects.
