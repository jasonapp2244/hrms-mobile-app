1 Executive Summary

The platform today is a working attendance system. This document sets out what is required to make it a professional Employment Management Portal — the standard expected of a commercial product sold to multiple organisations.

The recommendations fall into four bands:

🛡️ Must-fix now

  • Permanent attendance retention
  • Deletion safety (soft delete)
  • Login brute-force protection
  • Production configuration

🏢 Completes the product

  • Leave Management
  • Holiday calendar
  • Manager hierarchy & approvals
  • Attendance correction + audit

💰 Commercial value

  • Overtime & hours calculation
  • Payroll-ready exports
  • Automated reporting

📱 Market reach

  • Mobile API layer
  • Android & iOS apps
  • Push notifications
  • AI HR assistant
Two items need immediate attention. First, deleting an employee currently erases that person's entire attendance history permanently — a compliance and payroll-dispute risk in any organisation. Second, there is no way to issue an employee a login from the dashboard, so nobody added since initial setup can reach the self-service portal. Both are small fixes and both should be closed before the system holds real staff data.

Strategic choice. Not all 31 modules should be built. The realistic options are (a) deep attendance specialist — Tier A in full plus payroll export, competing on accuracy, proof-of-presence and field-staff coverage; or (b) full HR suite — all tiers, a materially larger investment competing directly with established platforms. Tier A is where the product is already strongest and where the remaining work is cheapest.

12 Feasibility & Constraints

Every module in this document has been checked against the current technology stack (PHP 8.2, Laravel 12, MySQL). The conclusion: all of it is buildable — but seven items carry a condition that should be settled before they are scheduled.

12.1 Buildable with no new dependency — 24 of 31

Standard application development on the existing database. Nothing to purchase, no external service, no hardware. This includes the entire employee lifecycle tier, all workplace operations, timesheets, travel, IP whitelist, expenses, loans and benefits.

Two components are already paid for and idle. The PDF engine used for attendance reports also produces ID cards and badges; and a QR-code library remains installed from the original design, unused since the check-in method changed — it is exactly what badge generation requires.

12.2 Buildable with a condition — 7 modules

ModuleCondition to resolve first
Face / selfie punchAutomatic face matching needs either a paid cloud service (per-use cost, and an internet dependency on every punch) or on-device matching, which requires the mobile app to exist first. Recommended: ship photo capture with an HR review gallery immediately — roughly two days — and treat automatic matching as a separate decision.
Biometric devicesRequires physical hardware to develop against — this cannot be built unseen. Also a deployment decision: a cloud-hosted system cannot reach devices inside a client's private network without a small on-site sync agent.
Field-staff trackingDepends on the mobile app. Apple restricts background location and rejects applications without a clearly documented business justification. Also the most privacy-sensitive feature proposed.
Payroll engineNot a technical constraint — a jurisdictional one. Tax and social-insurance rules differ by country and change annually. A target country must be chosen, and the rules carry an ongoing yearly maintenance obligation.
Statutory complianceRequires local legal and accounting input. Budget for an advisor alongside development.
Single sign-onGoogle and Microsoft are straightforward. Enterprise SAML additionally needs a client identity provider to test against.
Chat integrationSlack and Teams are straightforward. WhatsApp requires provider approval and carries a per-message charge.

12.3 Regulatory considerations

Biometric and location data are legally special. Face data and employee location are treated as sensitive personal data in most jurisdictions, typically requiring explicit consent, a stated retention period and a right of withdrawal. Employers have faced significant claims over biometric timeclocks specifically. Neither feature is blocked — but each ships with a consent flow and a written policy, and that should be inside the estimate rather than discovered afterwards.

12.4 Hosting decisions to settle early

AreaConsideration
Background processingReminders, automatic absence marking and scheduled reports need a continuously running worker. Basic shared hosting often disallows this; a scheduled fallback works but should inform the hosting choice.
Live updatesGenuinely instant screens need websocket support, commonly blocked on shared hosting. A short refresh interval is an adequate substitute at this scale — but "real-time" should not be promised on shared hosting.
Photo storageA photograph per employee per punch grows quickly. Production should use cloud object storage rather than server disk.
The real constraint is capacity, not technology. The full programme is approximately 275 development days — around one developer-year — on a codebase that currently has no automated test coverage. The risk is not in any single feature; it is in expanding an untested foundation. The test suite should therefore be funded before the expansion, not after it.

15 Accounts & Services Required

What the organisation must provide. None of these are built by the development team — each is an account to register, a subscription to purchase, or an asset to supply. They are grouped by when they become blocking, so nothing is bought before it is needed.

Ownership matters more than cost. Every account below should be registered under a company email address that the organisation controls — never a developer's personal account. If the Google Play or Apple account is held personally, transferring it later is slow, occasionally impossible, and the organisation cannot publish app updates without that individual. This is the most common and most costly administrative mistake in mobile projects.
