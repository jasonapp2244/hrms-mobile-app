What each role can do — web dashboard and mobile app

The four roles in KEMP, what each one reaches on the web, what the same person gets on their phone, and the boundaries that are held deliberately. Compiled from the permission seeder, the route gates and the app's own tab logic — not from a specification.

4 roles · 19 permissions Web dashboard + Flutter app Compiled 14 September 2026

How to read this

Three things are worth knowing before the tables.

A role is not a job title — it is a set of permissions. Access is decided by permission, never by role name, in every screen. The roles below are named bundles of those permissions. One person can hold more than one.
Manager is always held in addition to Employee, never on its own. A manager is a member of staff who also approves for their own reports — they clock in and book leave like everybody else. Read the Manager section as "Employee, plus the following".
The app is not a smaller copy of the web. Some things are on the web on purpose and will not appear on a phone however senior the account. Where that is a decision rather than a gap, it is marked By decision and the reason is given.

E Employee

Self-service only. One permission — view-attendance — which means "my own attendance", not anybody else's.

On the web dashboard

  • Clock in and out, and start/end a break
  • Own dashboard — today, hours worked, this week
  • Leave — balances, apply, track, cancel a pending request
  • Attendance corrections — raise a request, withdraw it
  • Shift swaps — offer, accept, decline, cancel
  • Own profile and password

In the mobile app

  • Clock — punch in/out and breaks, with GPS recorded, and a queue that holds a punch taken with no signal
  • History — day rows, totals, attendance score, and corrections
  • Leave — balances, apply, track, withdraw
  • Schedule — the published roster
  • Profile — own documents, colleague directory, home address & emergency contact, password, language, fingerprint/face lock
  • Notification centre, and push when it is switched on
The app works with no signal. It opens on the last verified sign-in, reads the saved copy of profile, history and roster, and queues a punch to send when the phone is back on the network. Anything shown from a saved copy says so, with the date it was taken.
What an employee cannot see, deliberately: anybody else's attendance, leave, salary or personal details. The colleague directory shows name, department, job title and office and nothing more — phone and email only if the company switches that on.

M Manager

Everything an Employee has, plus a team. Four permissions: view-attendance, view-team, approve-leave, approve-swaps.

On the web dashboard — own /manager area

  • Manager dashboard — the team's day at a glance
  • Team list, and a page per direct report
  • Team attendance and the attendance log, any date
  • Team schedule — published roster, read-only
  • Approvals inbox — leave, first step; shift swaps
  • Team reports — scoped to their own reports
  • Everything in the employee portal, for themselves

In the mobile app — a Team tab appears

  • Approvals — approve or reject leave, with a note; clashes shown before deciding
  • In today — who is in, late, on leave or absent, for any past date
  • Roster — a week per direct report, published days only
  • Off — a month calendar of who is away, approved and pending
  • Plus all five employee tabs, for themselves
"Team" means direct reports, and only direct reports. It is read from the reporting line on the employee record. It does not include a reporting sub-tree: a manager two levels up is not in the approval chain and does not see those records.
The Team tab needs a team. It appears only when the account both holds approve-leave and has somebody reporting to them. A manager with nobody assigned sees no Team tab rather than an empty one.
What a manager cannot do, deliberately:
  • No final leave approval. Leave is manager, then HR. A manager passes it up.
  • No attendance corrections. Deciding a correction is HR's alone, on the web.
  • No roster editing. They read the published roster; building it stays with manage-shifts. By decision
  • No personal details. Date of birth, home address, national ID, salary, documents and emergency contact are HR-grade and are not shown to a manager, even for their own team.
  • No company-wide anything. Every query behind this role is scoped to their reports.
  • No announcements. Broadcasting to a department or office is HR's. By decision

HR HR

Day-to-day people operations, company-wide. Fifteen permissions — everything except company-level configuration.

On the web dashboard

  • Employees — full records, create, edit, deactivate, photos, bulk import, export, org chart, onboarding/offboarding checklists
  • Document vault — contracts, ID, certificates, with expiry chasing
  • Attendance — logs, the live "who's in" board, manual entry and correction with an audit reason, regularisation decisions
  • Leave — the register, final approval, leave types, holidays, balances, accrual, team leave calendar
  • Shifts & roster — shifts, break policy, the weekly planner with drag-and-drop, rotations, publish/withdraw
  • Reports — late, overtime, payroll, department, weekly, the custom builder, plus PDF/Excel export and scheduled email delivery
  • Announcements — compose and send to everyone, a department or an office
  • Departments and designations

In the mobile app

  • The employee screens only — clock, history, leave, schedule, profile
  • HR clocks in and books their own leave like anybody else
  • No Team tab, and no HR approvals inbox

By decision

HR is desk-only on the phone, and that is intended rather than missing. Everything HR actually does — employee records, the leave register, reports, the document vault — is desk work. The company-wide stage-two approval queue stays on the dashboard, where the record it decides on is visible. If phone approvals for HR are wanted later, that is an addition, not a repair.
What HR cannot reach: company profile, offices and geofences, roles and permissions, and system settings. Those stay with the Admin.

A Administrator

Everything HR has, plus the configuration of the system itself. All nineteen permissions.

On the web dashboard

  • Everything listed under HR, and in addition:
  • Company profile — name, logo, address, timezone
  • Offices — branches, GPS coordinates, geofence radius
  • Roles & permissions — the editor for all of the above
  • Settings & policies — working week, reminder windows, geofence enforcement, idle timeout, 2FA requirement, directory contact visibility
  • Security trail — sign-ins, failures, lockouts, timeouts, password and settings changes
  • App crash reports

In the mobile app

  • Signs in successfully — and then every employee screen is refused, because an administrator account normally has no employee record behind it
  • An admin who is also a member of staff — with an employee record linked — gets the ordinary employee screens like anybody else

By design

An employee record and a login are separate things. Plenty of staff are on the payroll and have never signed in; an administrator account may exist with nobody on the payroll behind it. The app is built around the employee record, which is why an admin-only login has nothing to show there. Link the account to an employee record and the app behaves normally.
Who may create a login, and at what level. HR can create sign-in accounts — onboarding is their job — but only at employee or manager level. Promoting somebody to HR or Admin needs manage-roles, which only an Admin holds. Without that split, HR could mint an account, make it an admin and sign in as one. The same rule runs in reverse: HR cannot demote an existing administrator.

Manager holds a subset of HR's permissions, but sees far less. The three they share — view-team, approve-leave, approve-swaps — are the same permissions applied to different data: HR's screens are company-wide, the manager's are filtered to their own reports before anything is read. The permission opens the door; the scope decides what is in the room.

Boundaries held on purpose

The six that come up most often. Each is a decision, not an oversight — worth knowing before anyone reports them as bugs.

The boundaryWhy it is there
HR has no approvals inbox in the app HR's work is desk work, and the final approval is made against a record that is only fully visible on the dashboard.
A manager cannot correct attendance A correction changes the record a payroll was calculated from. It carries an audit reason and belongs to HR.
A manager cannot edit the roster Building and publishing the roster is one job held by manage-shifts. Managers read the published version — the same one their staff see.
A manager sees no personal details Date of birth, address, national ID, salary, documents and the emergency contact are HR-grade. A colleague cannot see more than a manager, and a manager cannot see these.
Scope is direct reports, not a sub-tree Leave approval is one hop — manager, then HR. Giving a manager two levels up sight of records they can never act on would be data without a purpose.
An admin login alone cannot use the app The app is built around the employee record. An account with none has no attendance, no leave and no schedule to show. Link one and it works.
